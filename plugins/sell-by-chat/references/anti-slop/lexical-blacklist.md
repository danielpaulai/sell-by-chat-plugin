# Lexical Blacklist — Words and Phrases

Merges the 50-section master blacklist with the LLM focal words identified in
corpus research (the Kobak et al. "why does ChatGPT delve so much" analysis of
excess word frequency in scientific English post-2022, and the Max Planck work
on lexical spikes since 2022).

**How to use this file:** do not read it while writing. Write first, then scan
the draft against it. Deleting a banned phrase is step one; step two is asking
what the sentence was actually trying to say and writing that instead.

**Override:** a client's genuine Voice DNA beats this list. If they say "at the
end of the day" out loud, in voice notes, unprompted — it is their voice. Only
the lexical gate is overridable. Structure, specificity, and evidence gates are
not.

## 1. Focal words — highest priority

These carry the strongest statistical signal. Corpus analysis found each spiked

foster · tapestry · realm · navigate · landscape · pivotal · resonate ·
testament · underscore · underscores · showcasing · showcase · compelling ·

## 2. Banned opening patterns

"In today's [anything]" · "In a world where" · "Imagine a world" · "Picture
this" · "Ever wondered" · "Have you ever stopped to think" · "What if I told
you" · "Let me ask you something" · "Here's the thing" · "The truth is" ·
"Let's be honest" · "Let me break it down for you" · "At the end of the day" ·
"When it comes to [topic]" · "In the realm of" · "In the landscape of" · "In
the age of" · "As we navigate" · "It's no secret that" · "We live in a time
when" · "Gone are the days when" · "The era of [X] is over" · "Welcome to the
age of" · "In an increasingly [adjective] world" · "As [industry] continues to
evolve" · "With the rise of [trend]" · "In recent years" · "More than ever
before" · "Now more than ever" · "It's [year] and" · "Let's talk about [topic]"

**What to do instead:** open in the middle of something. A specific moment, a
specific number, a specific thing someone said. See `copy-standards.md` §Hooks.

## 3. Banned power verbs

unlock · unleash · revolutionize · transform · optimize · maximize · leverage ·
harness · elevate · amplify · skyrocket · supercharge · turbocharge · catapult ·
propel · empower · enable · facilitate · streamline · accelerate · scale (when
not literal) · dominate · crush (goals) · nail (outcomes) · master · conquer ·
boost · enhance · implement · execute · deploy · utilize (use "use") · generate ·
drive (results) · deliver (value) · cultivate · foster · integrate · consolidate ·
monetize · capitalize · disrupt · innovate (as verb) · spearhead · orchestrate ·
curate · democratize · future-proof · supercharge · reimagine · redefine

**The pattern behind the pattern:** these are all *category verbs*. They name the
shape of an action without naming the action. "We streamlined onboarding" tells
you nothing. "We cut onboarding from nine forms to two" tells you everything.

## 5. Banned transitions and connectors

"That being said" · "With that in mind" · "Moving forward" · "Going forward" ·
"At the end of the day" · "When all is said and done" · "The bottom line is" ·
"What this means is" · "In other words" · "To put it simply" · "Simply put" ·
"Here's the deal" · "Here's what this means for you" · "Now, here's where it
gets interesting" · "But here's the kicker" · "But wait, there's more" · "And
here's why that matters" · "This is where things get [adjective]" · "That said"
· "Having said that" · "This brings us to" · "Which leads us to" · "This brings
me to my next point" · "Speaking of which" · "On that note" · "Before we move
on" · "Now that we've covered" · "As I mentioned earlier" · "As we discussed" ·
"To that end" · "In that regard" · "For that reason"

**Single-word connectors to cut on sight:** moreover · furthermore · however
(at sentence start, repeatedly) · additionally · consequently · therefore ·
thus · hence · indeed · notably · crucially · importantly · ultimately ·
fundamentally · essentially · arguably · undoubtedly · certainly

**Why:** uniform, formulaic transitions at fixed intervals are one of the most
reliable diagnostic markers. Expert human writing connects ideas through
*content* — the next sentence picks up a word or a fact from the last one — not
through signposts.

## 10. Banned fake credibility

"As someone who has [done thing]" · "In my X years of experience" · "I've seen
firsthand that" · "Time and time again, I've witnessed" · "Studies show" ·
"Experts agree" · "Science tells us" · "Research has proven" · "It's been
proven that" · "There's overwhelming evidence that" · "Everyone knows that" ·
"We all know that" · "Let's face it" · "The reality is" · "Make no mistake" ·
"Don't get me wrong" · "To be clear" · "For the record" · "Truth be told" · "If
I'm being honest" · "I'll be straight with you" · "Let me be frank" · "Between
you and me" · "Industry insiders know" · "The data doesn't lie" · "Numbers
don't lie" · "You can't argue with results" · "Track record speaks for itself"

**The rule:** credibility is demonstrated, never announced. Instead of "in my
years of experience," give the thing the years produced — the specific mistake,
the specific number, the specific client situation. A person who has actually
done it does not need to say so; the detail says it.

**Statistics:** any number without a source, a date, and a denominator is a
banned phrase. Round numbers (80%, 90%, 3×, 10×) are presumed invented until
sourced.

**Examples:** Apple, Nike, Amazon, Netflix/Blockbuster, Kodak, Airbnb, Uber as
illustrative examples are banned. Everyone uses them, which means they carry no
information. Use an example from the actual niche, or one the reader has not
heard used this way.

## 11. Banned urgency and manipulation

"Don't miss out on" · "Before it's too late" · "Time is running out" · "Limited
time only" · "Act now" · "This could be your last chance" · "While others are
[sleeping/waiting/hesitating]" · "The opportunity of a lifetime" · "This
changes everything" · "Nothing will ever be the same" · "Your life is about to
change" · "Prepare to be amazed" · "You won't believe what happens next" ·
"Mind-blowing" · "Life-changing" · "Can't afford to ignore" · "Imagine missing
this" · "Don't be the one who" · "While your competitors are" · "Everyone else
is already" · "You're being left behind" · "The world is passing you by" ·
"Wake up and smell the" · "It's time you knew the truth about"

**The distinction:** real urgency is allowed and works. Real urgency has a
mechanism — a date, a cap, a price change, a cohort that closes. State the
mechanism. Manufactured urgency has none, and the reader can tell.

## 12. Banned intensity modifiers and vague quantifiers

**Intensity:** very · really · truly · genuinely · absolutely · completely ·
totally · literally · incredibly · extremely · highly · deeply · profoundly ·
significantly · substantially · dramatically · remarkably · exceptionally ·
utterly · thoroughly · immensely · vastly · massively · hugely

**Quantifiers:** many · several · various · numerous · countless · a multitude
of · a myriad of · a plethora of · a host of · a range of · a number of ·
some · most · often · frequently · typically · generally · usually ·
increasingly · a growing number of · more and more

**The fix is the same for both:** replace with the number, or delete. "Many
clients" → "eleven clients." "Significantly faster" → "four minutes instead of
forty." If you do not have the number, "some clients" is more honest than
"many," and no adverb is more honest than either.

## 13. Banned meta-commentary

"In this post, I'll cover" · "By the end of this article, you'll know" · "Let's
dive in" · "Let's get into it" · "First, let's define" · "Before we begin" ·
"Now that we've established" · "As mentioned above" · "As we'll see" · "More on
that later" · "But first" · "Quick note:" · "Side note:" · "Disclaimer:" ·
"TL;DR" (as a substitute for writing tightly) · "Here's a breakdown" · "Let me
explain" · "Let me walk you through" · "I'll keep this short" · "Long story
short"

**Why:** meta-commentary is the writer narrating the act of writing. It adds
words and subtracts trust. Just do the thing.

## 14. Banned sentence starters

Starting three or more sentences the same way is itself a violation. Beyond
that, these specific starters:

"The reality is" · "The fact is" · "The thing is" · "Here's what" · "This is
why" · "That's because" · "What's more" · "Even better" · "Best of all" ·
"And that's not all" · "The result?" · "The outcome?" · "The problem?" · "The
solution?" · "Sound familiar?" · "Think about it" · "Consider this" · "Picture
it" · "Enter [X]" · "Cue [X]" · "Spoiler alert" · "Plot twist" · "Fun fact"

**One-word fragment starters** used as a rhythm device — "Simple." "Brutal."
"Wild." "Insane." "Exactly." — are banned when they appear more than once in a
piece, and banned entirely if they carry no content.

## 15. Banned sentence enders

"...and that's a good thing." · "...and that matters." · "...which is exactly
the point." · "...and it works." · "...period." · "...full stop." · "...every
single time." · "...without exception." · "...guaranteed." · "...simple as
that." · "...end of story." · "...you name it." · "...and more." · "...and
beyond." · "...to say the least." · "...if you know, you know."

**Participial tails** — a clause hanging off the end that gestures at
significance without adding any: "...highlighting the importance of consistent
messaging," "...underscoring the need for a robust strategy," "...reflecting a
broader shift in the industry," "...demonstrating the value of." These are pure
filler. Structural detail in `structural-tells.md`.

## 19. Banned dialogue and drama devices

**Fake dialogue:** "A client said to me last week..." followed by dialogue no
human speaks. Real quotes have hesitation, incomplete grammar, and specifics.
If you did not record it, paraphrase and say you are paraphrasing.

**Dramatic pauses as formatting:** a line break before a one-word sentence for
effect, used more than once. Ellipses used to build suspense. A single word on
its own line. "Wait for it."

**Manufactured reveals:** "Then something happened." · "And then it hit me." ·
"That's when I realised." · "Everything changed."

## 20. Banned instructional patterns

"Here's how to [X] in [N] simple steps" · "Step 1: ... Step 2: ..." when the
steps are not sequential · "The [N]-step framework" where N was chosen before
the steps · "First... Second... Finally..." · "Follow these [N] rules" ·
"Master these [N] skills" · "Avoid these [N] mistakes"

**The underlying rule:** the number comes from the content. If there are four
things, say four. Do not pad to five or cut to three because odd numbers feel
better. Forcing content into a pre-chosen bucket is one of the clearest
structural tells.

## 21. The AI vocabulary register — the deeper problem

Deleting listed words is surface work. The register underneath is the real tell,
and it has three properties:

**Elevated diction where plain would do.** *utilize* for use, *facilitate* for
help, *individuals* for people, *purchase* for buy, *commence* for start,
*endeavour* for try, *methodology* for method, *ideation* for ideas,
*optimisation* for making it better. LLMs are trained heavily on formal written
English and default to its register even in casual contexts.

**Elegant variation.** Avoiding repeating a word by cycling through synonyms —
"the platform... the solution... the tool... the offering... the system." Human
writers repeat the noun. Repetition of the right word reads as confidence;
synonym-cycling reads as a thesaurus.

**Nominalisation cascades.** Turning verbs into abstract nouns and stacking
them: "the implementation of an optimisation strategy for the enhancement of
engagement." This masks agency — nobody is doing anything — and it is where most
corporate slop lives. Unwind it: who did what to whom.

**The register test:** read the sentence out loud. If you would not say it to
a person in a room, do not write it. That single test catches more slop than
any word list.
