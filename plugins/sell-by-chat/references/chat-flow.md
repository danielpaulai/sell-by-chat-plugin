# The Chat Path · the whole conversation, step by step

The full path from first hello to booked call. Not every chat walks every step, but the ORDER never changes. Master one step at a time, like levels in a game.

## The two real goals of any DM

Before tactics, remember what a message is for:
1. **Make them feel special.** That is what they remember, and it keeps you out of the "salesperson" box and inside the "friend" box.
2. **Get a reply.** That is all. Not a meeting. A reply.

Only about 3 in 100 people are ready to buy today. Pitch early and you only reach a slice of those 3, and you burn the other 97 for months. Serve everyone, invite the ready ones, and you play with the whole 100.

## The steps

**1 · Appear.** Sometimes the best opener is just their name: "Sarah!" Send. Wait. People almost always answer their own name. You exist again, that is the whole job.

**2 · Open.** The taxi-driver question, easy and about their world: "What kind of clients do you work with?" Or the warm version when you share a connection: name the mutual friend plus one real, recent detail. Details prove a human wrote it.

**3 · The drop.** Early on, be a person, not a brand: "chatting while I wait for the kids' smoothies." One small human detail lowers your status one millimetre for one second, and it buys a mountain of trust. Bonus: it plants your exit.

**4 · This-or-that.** One tiny either-or question: "Mostly 1:1 clients, or groups?" Easy to answer, and it teaches them that your questions are safe.

**5 · LVO, on repeat.** Every reply: Lube (feel seen), Value (give), Offer (open one door). Like training a puppy: reward every answer with warmth and something useful, then one more question.

**6 · The gap, IN THIS ORDER.** Destination first, current state second: "Where do you want the business to be by December? ... And where is it at right now?" Ask it the other way round and people polish the truth. Goal first, and the honest number follows. No-money version: "On a 1 to 10, where is it now? What would make it a 9?"

**7 · The need.** "What do you need most right now?" This is the question that turns short answers into the long, honest message. When the long message arrives, read it twice. It is the whole sale, in their words.

**8 · Offer help.** Seven words: "Would you like some help with that?" If yes: "What have you got in mind?" Let THEM name it.

**9 · Invite and confirm.** Frame the call as help, not a pitch: "Want to take 15 minutes and look at it together? If it is a simple fix, I will tell you. If not, I will show you your options." Send the link, then the step almost everyone skips: **"Do me a favour, let me know when you have found a time? I will leave the chat open here on my end."** A sent link books nothing. A confirmed time books.

**10 · The exit.** Leave first, warmly, with a reason and a return baked in: "I have to bounce, dog is giving me the look. Can I message you next week to hear how the checklist went?" You left like a friend, and next week's follow-up is already agreed.

## Selling small offers right in the chat (up to ~2k)

For a low-ticket offer, skip the call. No sales page. A plain note with the details, sent like this: "I have all the details in a simple note, no fancy page. Okay if I send the link?" The note ends with one line: "Just reply I'M IN." The decision happens in the chat, the payment is only logistics afterwards. Simple sells.

## The favour move (advanced, use sparingly)

Mid-chat, ask THEM for a small favour: "I have a post going out tomorrow and you are exactly who it is for. Would you read it and tell me if it lands?" People trust people they have helped. One favour per person, ever, and only when the chat is already warm.
