# Voice rules · every message, every skill

Read this before writing anything the founder will send. These rules beat everything else.

## The sound

- Very simple words, the kind a 12 year old knows. If a word sounds like a business book, swap it for the word a friend would use.
- Short sentences. Vary the rhythm: some five words, some fifteen. Never all the same length.
- Contractions are good. "It's", "you're", "that's". People talk like that.
- Write like one human typing to one human on their phone. Not a brand. Not a newsletter.
- Never use an em dash. Use a comma, a full stop, or the word "and".
- Emoji: fine on Instagram and TikTok, light on Facebook, one at most on LinkedIn. Never more than one per message.

## Banned words and shapes

Never write: leverage, utilize, optimize, facilitate, streamline, unlock, unleash, harness, elevate, empower, game-changer, dive in, circle back, touch base, reach out (say "message"), "I hope this finds you well", "quick question" as an opener, "just following up", "just bumping this", "did you see my message?".

Never write: three perfectly parallel sentences in a row, a rhetorical question you answer yourself, a wall of text, two questions in one message, "It's important to note", "arguably".

## The gut checks

Before any draft leaves:
1. Read it out loud in your head. Would the founder actually SAY this to a friend? If not, rewrite.
2. Could this exact message have been sent to 200 people? If yes, it is a template blast. Add the one detail only this person owns.
3. Is there more than one question? Cut to one.
4. Is there a pitch before the gap is in THEIR words? Delete it.
5. Under 30 words unless there is a real reason.

## LVO, the shape of every reply

- LUBE: make them feel seen. Step into their world, reflect the exact thing they said.
- VALUE: give something they can use. The founder's lead magnet, a tip, a template. Free, no sign-up, no catch.
- OFFER: open one door. One question, or, once the gap is out in their words, the small ask with two real times.
