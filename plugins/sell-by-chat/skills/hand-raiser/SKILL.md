# Hand-Raiser: one post, many chat starts

A hand-raising post exists for one reason: to make the right person raise their hand so a conversation can start. Keep the user's ratio honest: about 80 percent give posts, 20 percent ask posts. One or two hand-raisers a week, never daily.

## The proven shapes (adapt, never copy blindly)

- **The classic**: "Are you still looking for [more clients / RESULT]?"
- **The freebie**: "Would you like a free copy of my [guide / checklist / template] that helps you X? Comment WORD and I will send it over."
- **This or that**: "Are you looking for [option A], or something more [option B]?"
- **The 5-for-30**: "I am looking for 5 [target people] who want [result] in the next 30 days. Want in? Comment or DM me."
- **The class**: "We are starting a new [N-week] class to help [people] get [result]. Comment below for details."

## Flow

1. Read the user's `VOICE-DNA.md` (current folder, then home) and, if present, their niche or offer notes. Ask for their niche and offer in one line if unknown.
2. Ask which shape they want, or pick the best fit for their situation and say why.
3. Write the post: short, plain, in their voice, one tiny ask, no jargon, no em dashes, no hashtag walls.
4. Add the follow-through plan: everyone who comments gets a DM the same day ("Hey NAME, saw your comment, sending it over now") and the freebie goes ungated.
5. Remind them: if 50 sends or one week of posts gets zero hands, that is data, not failure. Change the words and go again.

## Platform register

Read `SELL-BY-CHAT.md` (current folder) for the founder's platform, give, ask and file paths. Match the platform's native register: LinkedIn is professional but human; Instagram is casual, emoji welcome, story replies count; Facebook is neighborly, Messenger tone; TikTok is fast and playful, reply in comments first, then DM. Same method everywhere, different doorbell.

## Words

Very simple words, the kind a 12 year old knows. Short sentences. If a word sounds like a business book, swap it for the word a friend would use. Never use an em dash.
